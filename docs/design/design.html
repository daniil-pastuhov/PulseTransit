Fjord

The PulseTransit board as it is today, made consistent: a clear blue on cool slate, one sturdy grotesk with tabular figures, and colour that only appears where it means something. Three rules guide every screen: the next departure is the loudest thing, numbers never jump (tabular figures, fixed time column), and the wall stays calm (no motion, no glare, OLED black at night).

Color primitives

Raw ramps. Screens never use these directly, only through the semantic roles below. Line colours follow the Norwegian operators' hues, adjusted so white text passes 4.5:1 on every pill in both themes.

Semantic roles

What screens use. App-specific roles: now (a departure leaving now), late (a delayed time), route/on-route/route-accent (the fastest-route banner, deep blue in dark so it doesn't glare on the wall), and one line-* pair per mode.

Contrast (WCAG 2.2 AA)

Every foreground/background pair the components use, generated by the build from tokens.json, so it can't drift.

Typography

Public Sans, bundled in both apps and the widgets so Android, iOS and desktop match. Every number uses tabular figures. clock, countdown and pill are the board's own styles; on the wall (≥ 700 px wide) the clock grows to 64 and countdowns to 24.

Spacing

Shape

Small radius for pills and chips, medium for fields and callouts, large for cards and the route banner, xl for sheets and dialogs.

Elevation

Light: soft shadows. Dark: hairline outlines instead, since shadows vanish on OLED black.

Icons

Material Symbols Rounded, outlined, weight 400. On iOS the same glyphs map to SF Symbols (bus, tram, tram.fill.tunnel, train.side.front.car, ferry, figure.walk, cloud.rain…). Mode icons are never the only signal: the line pill's colour and the destination carry it too.

Illustrations

Used only in empty states, onboarding and screen-level errors. Flat shapes in role colours: a stop sign, a departure board, a route line.

Line pill

The line's public code on its mode colour. Min width 44 so 1–4 characters align in a column; longer codes ("FLY24") grow. Never truncated.

Screen readers hear "Bus 30", not "30": the pill carries aria-label with the mode.

Departure row

Pill · mode icon · destination · delay chip · time. Time format: Now → "4 min" up to 15 min → clock time after. The time column is right-aligned with tabular figures so it never jumps. Rows are buttons: tapping opens departure details.

Error and feedback framework

Every message lands on exactly one surface. Walk the list in order and stop at the first match:

  1. T6 dialog: a decision is needed first. Rare here: nothing in PulseTransit is irreversible.
  2. T1 field error: tied to one input (coordinates, schedule days).
  3. T5 screen error: the screen's main content can't be shown and nothing is cached. On the board this is per stop panel, so one failing stop never blanks the others.
  4. T4 callout: a lasting condition (offline with old data, location off, update available). On the wall it's the only error surface, because nobody is there to read a snackbar.
  5. T3 snackbar: a transient event in an app someone is holding: "Line 31 hidden · Undo", "Saved Majorstuen to destinations".

A failed background refresh while data is still fresh shows nothing. After 2 missed refresh intervals it becomes the T4 "shown data is N min old" callout. With no cache at all it is T5 in each panel.

Choose your stops

Open Settings to add up to 6 stops, and pick which directions of each to show. When they don’t all fit, the board takes turns showing them.

Step 1 of 4

Fastest routes

Add up to 5 destinations in Settings to see the fastest way there from the board’s location, and choose when each one is shown, like weekday mornings.

Step 2 of 4

Tap for details

Tap a departure to see its stops and later times, or to hide a line you never take. Tap a route to see every step and get alerts along the way.

Step 3 of 4

Made to stay on

Settings has burn-in protection, dimming when no one is around, waking by camera motion and a theme that follows the light in the room.

Step 4 of 4

Departures from where you are

The nearest stop’s departures show on their own and follow you as you move. Tap the stop’s name to pick another one nearby.

Step 1 of 3

Routes from right here

Add the places you often go to in Settings, and each gets its next routes from where you stand. For a one-off trip, tap the search button.

Step 2 of 3

Make room

Drag the bar between routes and departures to give either one more space.

Step 3 of 3

Use your location

On the spot finds the nearest stop and plans routes from where you stand. Your position is sent only to Entur’s journey planner, to answer those searches.

  • Precise location gives the nearest stop; approximate still works, with a wider search.
  • You can turn it off at any time in your phone’s settings.

On the spot needs your location

Location access is needed to show routes and stops from where you are. Turn it on in your phone’s settings, or set a fixed location instead.

Wake screen by front camera motion

Wake the screen when someone walks by

While the screen is dimmed, the front camera watches for movement and wakes the board.

  • Frames never leave this device. Each one is compared with the last and thrown away.
  • Nothing is recorded, saved or sent anywhere.
  • Choose how much motion wakes it in Settings, under Camera Sensitivity.
Wake screen by front camera motion

Camera access is off

PulseTransit can’t use the front camera, so the board won’t wake by motion. It still dims when the room is quiet and wakes when you touch it.

Home-screen widgets

Three Android (Glance) widgets, drawn at their real launcher size on a home screen. Primary question: “When do I need to go?”, answered without opening the app. A widget is one flat surface (no blur, no shadow: RemoteViews draws neither reliably), line pills as on the board, and one footer line that says how old the data is. Times are clock times, not countdowns: a widget redraws every 15 min, so “4 min” would be wrong a minute later. Accent colour and a dot mark realtime; plain is timetable; struck through is cancelled. Tapping anywhere opens the board; only the refresh icon is a separate target (drawn at 20, 48 to touch).

For iOS WidgetKit later: 2×2 = small, 4×2 = medium, 4×4 = large; 4×1 and 4×3 have no iOS home-screen size (4×1 maps best to the lock-screen rectangular accessory). iOS 15–16 widgets cannot hold buttons, so the refresh icon is dropped there (the timeline reloads itself) and the header title gets the full width. The picker is Android only: on iOS the system edit sheet lists the same choices from an intent configuration. The weather and mode icons are bundled Material Symbols drawables on Android (Glance Image) and SF Symbols or the same assets on iOS, replacing today’s emoji.
